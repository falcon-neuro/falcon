# UI design

How the whole app looks: its colours, type, shapes and the widgets drawn from them, on every screen. What a screen does is not here: the designer's behaviour is in [pipeline_designer.md](pipeline_designer.md). This file is the look's spec followed by its open questions: a question that is settled moves up into the spec.

## Asked for
- **A 1950s atomic-age look, everywhere**, asked for by the user on 23 November 2026. The user's words that day, in order: "try to make the design of falcon ui skeumorphic, like oscilloscopes, amplifiers etc"; then "pls keep things as if they are developed in silicon valley, high level ui ux, maximum user satisfaction", "ui is shit now" and "i want 50s atomic age design everywhere, i mean everywhere, GO!".
- **Only the look changes** (the user, that day, once the work had started: "you only change ui, not ux, just the pixels and colors, no logic"). So the redesign changes how a thing is drawn, its colours, type, shapes, borders, shadows and ornament, and nothing else: which things a screen holds, where the layout puts them, what a click or a key does, every text, and all code that doesn't draw stay as they are. The tests of the UI pass unchanged.
- Claude's reading, not confirmed by the user: every screen and every widget of the app gets the look, to the standard of a commercial product; things may look like parts of an instrument of that time (a painted panel, a lamp, a knob's cap) as long as they are used exactly as before.
- **The layout too, where it looks like stock Dear ImGui** (the user, 23 November 2026, of the Processors panel and the options panel: "processors panel hurts my eyes", "options panel hurts my eyes", and, asked what hurts: "layout hurts, looks soo c++ imgui", "i want silicon valley level sleek design"). It lifts **Only the look changes** for where things stand inside a panel. Claude's reading, told to the user and not confirmed: spacing, alignment, the sizes of text and how a panel's parts are grouped may change; what a click or a key does and every text stay, so the tests of the UI still pass. What reads as stock ImGui in the two panels, Claude's, from two pictures: neither panel has a surface or an edge of its own; every text has one size and weight, so a title, a group's name and a row look alike; the tree's solid black triangles; rows and fields stacked at the left edge at ImGui's own spacing. Claude's proposal for a first step, taken by the user ("do it sleek, lets go"): the Processors panel as the sidebar of a product of today, e.g. Linear's or Figma's (see **Step 14** under **State of the work**); the options panel is to follow once the user has seen it.

- **Step 18, pictures from PNG, and the processors' icons in the Processors panel** (the user, 23 November 2026: "lets use the icons", "i have svg icons per processor, what to do?", and, told of three ways: "lets do png, keep the svgs for source, then get png output for 48x48 or whatever needed", "then we also use the png for the logo", "no tracing bullshit"; built that day, seen by Claude in pictures of the panel and of the welcome screen, shown to the user, no answer yet). **The SVGs are the source, and the app loads PNGs made from them.** `scripts/export_png.sh` writes them with Inkscape: each processor's icon from `assets/svg/<file name>.svg` to `assets/png/processors/<file name>.png`, 48 pixels square, and the logo to `assets/png/logo.png`, a square of 512 pixels (288 pixels wide at first, see **Step 19**). A picture is loaded when it is first drawn and kept as a texture (`src/ui/common/pictures.cpp`). Only how solid each pixel is comes from the file: the picture takes the colour it is drawn in, as an icon of the icon font does.
