# UI design

How the whole app looks: its colours, type, shapes and the widgets drawn from them, on every screen. What a screen does is not here: the designer's behaviour is in [pipeline_designer.md](pipeline_designer.md). This file is the look's spec followed by its open questions: a question that is settled moves up into the spec.

## Asked for
- **A 1950s atomic-age look, everywhere**, asked for by the user on 23 November 2026. The user's words that day, in order: "try to make the design of falcon ui skeumorphic, like oscilloscopes, amplifiers etc"; then "pls keep things as if they are developed in silicon valley, high level ui ux, maximum user satisfaction", "ui is shit now" and "i want 50s atomic age design everywhere, i mean everywhere, GO!".
- **Only the look changes** (the user, that day, once the work had started: "you only change ui, not ux, just the pixels and colors, no logic"). So the redesign changes how a thing is drawn, its colours, type, shapes, borders, shadows and ornament, and nothing else: which things a screen holds, where the layout puts them, what a click or a key does, every text, and all code that doesn't draw stay as they are. The tests of the UI pass unchanged.
- Claude's reading, not confirmed by the user: every screen and every widget of the app gets the look, to the standard of a commercial product; things may look like parts of an instrument of that time (a painted panel, a lamp, a knob's cap) as long as they are used exactly as before.

## State of the work
- It is done in the branch `ui-polish`, which left `refactor` on 23 November 2026, so that it and the other work on the app go on at once.
- **The branch follows `refactor`** (the user, that day: "follow what other branch is doing and merge those changes into yourself always"): before each step of the work, and whenever `refactor` has a new commit, that branch is merged into this one. Where both changed a file, `refactor`'s logic is kept whole and the new look is put onto it again; what `refactor` adds to a screen gets the new look too. For a merge this branch's work is committed, on this branch alone and never pushed.
- A first test of the branch, the window's background in black, was seen by the user in the app that day and taken out again.
- **Small steps, each shown to the user, who steers** (the user, 23 November 2026, once a plan of four long steps by many agents had started: "i want smaller amount of work and then i wanna see it and steer you, i dont want an update evet 30 mins, i want it every 2 mins"). A step is one change of the look that takes about two minutes, then pictures of it and one question. That replaces the plan of four steps (a list of all the app draws, a design chosen among several proposals, building, a review).
- Still running from that plan, in the background, and kept as reference: seven agents that list what each screen draws, and one that writes `src/tests/ui/gallery.py`, a tool that draws a picture of every state of the app. Until they end, a step's pictures are taken from a copy of the app of its own, `build/look_output/`, and written to `build/look/`.
- **Step 1, the colours alone** (built that day, seen by Claude in three pictures; the user: "colours are fine i like it"): a cream window, the cores' boxes in pale turquoise, blocks and cards in ivory, a selected block's outline in dark teal, text in a warm charcoal, fields in sand, the bar at the bottom in taupe while nothing is compiled. The colours of what a line carries keep their hues, samples blue, events orange, a state value green. Only values of colour constants changed, in `colours.cpp`, `falcon_ui.cpp`, `pipeline_designer.cpp` and `processors_panel.cpp`, and the window's colour and the text's became constants of `colours.cpp`.
- **Step 2, the shapes of buttons and fields** (built that day, seen by Claude in two pictures, shown to the user, no answer yet): every button and field has rounded corners (`FRAME_ROUNDING` in `colours.cpp`, 6 pixels) and more room around its text (10 pixels at the sides, 5 above and below), set once in ImGui's style in `falcon_ui.cpp`, with rounded scrollbars and popups. The bar on top and the rows that hold a button grew by 4 pixels with it. Steps 1 and 2 are linted and formatted; the tests of the UI haven't run on them yet.
- **The typeface stays Inter, and the look stays humble** (the user, 23 November 2026: "typeface is not needed, keep the current one, it needs to be not that fancy, humble, a bit boring scientific tool looking"). So the atomic age shows in the colours and the shapes, never in ornament that draws the eye from the work.
- **Step 3, a soft shadow** (built that day, seen by Claude in a picture; the user: "it's ok"): under each block, each card of the welcome screen and each problem's box, five nearly clear layers 2 pixels lower than the thing (`shadow.cpp` in `src/ui/common/`). The shadow is drawn in a lower layer of the window's drawing, so it lies under the thing's background. Linted and formatted.
- **Skeuomorphism all the way** (the user, 23 November 2026, asked whether the bars should get a plain painted look: "skeumorphism all the way!"). Claude's reading, with the user's earlier "humble, a bit boring scientific tool looking": parts look like the real parts of an instrument, lit from above, standing out or set in, without ornament.
- **Step 4, the bars and their keys** (built that day, seen by Claude in a picture, shown to the user, no answer yet): the bar on top and the bar at the bottom are painted strips, lighter at the top edge with a bright line along it, the bottom bar in the colour of its state as before; the top bar's three buttons, Pipelines, Run and Compile, are keys that stand out of it, with a darker rim under them, lighter under the mouse, and set in, their shading turned over, while pushed. In `instrument.cpp` of `src/ui/common/` (`paint_window_as_panel`, `instrument_key`). A key acts exactly as ImGui's button did: it is that button with its own colours made clear, the key drawn in a layer under it. ImGui's internal header, whose gradient function was meant for this, can't be included under the build's warnings, so the gradient is a function of Falcon's own. The bottom bar's buttons stay flat. Linted and formatted.
- **Step 5, fields set into the panel** (built that day, seen by Claude in a picture, shown to the user, no answer yet; the user had no preference, "idk", so Claude chose it as the keys' counterpart): every text field, number box and list to choose from has a darker outline, a shade along its upper inner edge and a light line under it, drawn over the field once ImGui has drawn it (`draw_last_item_as_sunken` in `instrument.cpp`). The text cursor, white until then and unseen on a light field, is the text's colour. Its outline and the shade were seen by the user, who answered "ok".
- **Step 6, the number box's step buttons as keys** (built that day, seen by Claude in a picture): `-` and `+` beside a number box are small keys like the top bar's.
- **Steps 4 to 6 are not the skeuomorphism asked for** (the user, 23 November 2026, on seeing them: "this is not realistic skeomorphism tho, this is 2005 digital ui design", "i want real analog design"). Gradients, rims and bevels drawn in flat colour read as a digital UI of 2005, not as an instrument. Claude's reading, not confirmed: real analog means the materials and parts of an instrument of the 1950s, e.g. a painted metal panel with its grain (hammertone), lettering printed or engraved on it, a bezel with screws, knobs and switches of bakelite, a pilot lamp behind coloured glass, a scope's screen; light that falls softly from one side with the shadows and highlights a real part has. In ImGui that needs textures made when the app starts (grain, noise), since flat colours and gradients alone give the 2005 look. Steps 1 to 6 stay in the code until the new direction replaces them.
- **Step 7, the window painted as hammered steel, taken out** (23 November 2026): the cream ground with the grain of hammered paint, made as a picture when the app starts, was shown to the user, who asked whether this was the analog look meant: "nope, remove it pls". It was removed the same day, its file with it. So a painted metal surface with grain isn't what the user means by real analog design.
- **Keep it 2026, sleek** (the user, 23 November 2026, on the pilot lamp of step 8: "you need to somehow keep it 2026 tho", "even if its a light, it should look sleek"). Claude's reading: parts of an instrument, drawn as a product of today would draw them, crisp, flat in their shading and precise, never glossy or cartoonish: no glass highlights, thick bezels or strong bevels.
- **Step 8, a status lamp for the pipeline's state** (built that day; seen by Claude in pictures before compiling, while compiling and once compiled; shown to the user, no answer yet): the bar at the bottom is the neutral panel colour with dark text, no longer filled with the state's colour; the state's colour is in a small lamp left of the state's text, a crisp dot of light in a thin ring a shade darker than the panel, with a faint halo around it when lit and a whiter centre. It is unlit, its colour faint, before anything is compiled, and lit in every other state (`draw_status_lamp` in `instrument.cpp`, `is_lamp_lit` of the bar's state in `pipeline_designer.cpp`). Its first version, a domed glass in a dark bezel with a highlight, was replaced the same day after the user's words above.
- **Step 9, flat and sleek** (the user, 23 November 2026: "2026, like sleekest possible"; built that day, seen by Claude in pictures, shown to the user, no answer yet): every gradient of steps 4 to 6 is gone. The bars are flat panels with a hairline along their top edge; a key has a flat face, near white, a hairline edge and a soft shadow of three faint layers under it, lighter under the mouse, a shade darker and without its shadow while pushed; a field has a hairline outline and a faint shade along its upper inner edge, with no light line under it.
- **Step 10, every text button a key** (the user, 23 November 2026: "keep it sleek, continue"; built that day, seen by Claude in pictures of the designer, the welcome screen with pipelines and the trash): Create, Trash, Rename, Cancel and Pipelines on the welcome screen, Restore, Delete for good, Delete and Keep in the trash, and in the designer "Show the list", "Show the raw output", "Put its own name back" and "Put the default back" (until then ImGui's blue) are keys as in step 9; those that were ImGui's small buttons are keys as low as a line of text (`instrument_small_key`). A button that is an icon alone, and the bottom bar's warning and Log, stay flat. All 154 tests of the UI pass on steps 1 to 10. 
- **Step 11, no shadow under a card, a fainter one under a block** (the user, 23 November 2026, on the welcome screen's cards: "that shadow is not nice", "also not slick enough"; built that day, seen by Claude in pictures): a card of the welcome screen and of the trash has no shadow, only its edge, which is a lighter hairline than before (`CARD_BORDER_COLOUR`). The list of cards clips what lies outside it, so a shadow wider than a card was cut off at hard edges and stood as a grey band between two cards. A block and a problem's box keep a shadow, now wider, fainter and neutral in colour: twelve layers, each nearly clear, 3 pixels lower than the block.
- Step 11 was seen by the user: "slick nice".
- **Step 12, tooltips** (built that day, seen by Claude in a picture of a card's icon with its tooltip): a tooltip has rounded corners and a hairline edge in the cards' edge colour, and its ground is the ivory of the cards (`TOOLTIP_BACKGROUND`), no longer pale yellow. ImGui gives a tooltip the rounding of a window and the border of a popup, so `WindowRounding` and `PopupBorderSize` are set in `falcon_ui.cpp`; the app's one window fills the screen, so its own rounding doesn't show.
- **What `refactor` brought on 23 November 2026 took the new colours without a change** (merged that day, seen by Claude in two pictures): a block's ports, the hops between cores and the rings are drawn with the colour constants that step 1 repainted. Where both branches had changed a block's drawing, the block keeps `refactor`'s reckoned height and this branch's shadow. All 180 tests of the UI pass on the merged branch.
- **Step 13, "Show ports" a key** (built that day, seen by Claude in a picture, not yet shown to the user): the button beside the line that names the isolated cores, which `refactor` brought as ImGui's small button, is a key as low as a line of text, as those of step 10. Linted and formatted; the 180 tests of the UI pass.

## Open questions
- **A ring's box and its slider keep ImGui's own look**, found on 23 November 2026 after the merge: the slider isn't set in as a field is (step 5), and its grab has ImGui's own colour, which no step set. The box opens on a click on a ring, so no picture shows it and Claude hasn't seen it.
- **Which instrument the app looks like**, asked of the user on 23 November 2026 after "i want real analog design": e.g. a Tektronix oscilloscope of the 1950s (blue-grey hammertone paint, white printed lettering, black knobs, a green screen), or a Hewlett-Packard instrument of the time (light grey panel, dark grey lettering, a cream dial). The whole palette follows from it, so it replaces the colours of step 1.
- **A block's type as its largest text**, which the user decided for the redesign on 17 November 2026 (see **What the designer does comes first, its look after** in pipeline_designer.md), changes which of a block's texts stands out. Whether that is still "just the pixels and colors" wasn't asked: a block's name stays the bold one until it is.
- **The website** has a look of its own (see website.md). Whether "everywhere" means it too wasn't asked; the work is on the app alone.
- **The desktop's title bar** on top of the window isn't Falcon's to draw yet (see **No title bar of the desktop's** under **Later** in pipeline_designer.md), so it keeps the desktop's look.
